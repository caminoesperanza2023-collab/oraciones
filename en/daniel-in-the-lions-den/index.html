Daniel in the Lions' Den: The Courage He Would Not Abandon

Daniel in the lions' den is a story of courage formed long before the danger arrived. Daniel would not hide his prayer, even when a royal decree made faithfulness costly.

Pray along with the video

Full text

Daniel chapter six says a royal decree forbade petitions to anyone except the king for thirty days.

When Daniel learned it had been signed, he went home. With his windows open toward Jerusalem, he knelt and prayed three times, as before. His enemies found him and forced the king to enforce the law. Daniel was lowered into the lions' den, and a stone sealed the entrance.

At dawn, the king found Daniel alive. Daniel said God had sent an angel and shut the lions' mouths.

Daniel's courage began before the den: in the daily faithfulness he refused to abandon. What does that teach you?

What the Bible says

Daniel chapter 6 records that he continued praying three times a day with his windows open toward Jerusalem, just as he had done before. His enemies used the decree against him, and Daniel was lowered into a den sealed with a stone. At dawn the king found him alive. Daniel testified that God had sent an angel and shut the lions' mouths.

What the Church teaches

Daniel 6 shows that Daniel's courage in the lions' den did not appear suddenly. His enemies knew they could only accuse him through his faithfulness to God. When Daniel learned that the decree had been signed, he continued his established practice of praying three times a day with his windows open toward Jerusalem. He did not create a public spectacle, but neither did he turn prayer into a secret to protect himself. The story also reveals the king's weakness: Darius was distressed, yet the law he had approved trapped him. Daniel was placed in the den, and a stone sealed its entrance. At dawn, Daniel testified that God sent an angel and shut the lions' mouths. Scripture presents this as God's deliverance in a particular event, not as a guarantee that believers will avoid every consequence or suffering. The deeper lesson is that faithful habits shape courage before a crisis. Prayer, truthfulness, and obedience train the heart to choose well under pressure. Read all of Daniel 6 and notice the contrast between manipulation, regret, steady prayer, and deliverance. Which daily practice is forming your choices now: prayer, Scripture, examination of conscience, service, or honest accountability?
