Your Candle Keeps Praying After You Leave

You light a candle in church, leave it there, and walk out. What exactly is that gesture for?

It isn't magic, it isn't a payment, and it isn't superstition.

At first it was pure necessity: the earliest Christians met at night and in catacombs, and they needed light. But very quickly the light stopped being merely practical. In the liturgy the flame came to stand for Christ — which is why at the Easter Vigil the church starts in darkness and the deacon sings «Light of Christ» three times while the whole building lights up, candle by candle, from a single flame.

What the video says

You light a candle in church, leave it there, and walk out. What exactly is that gesture for? It isn't magic. It isn't a payment, and it isn't superstition. What it actually means is far better. At first, it was pure necessity. [music] The earliest Christians met at night and in catacombs, and they needed light.

But very quickly, the light stopped being merely practical. In the liturgy, the flame came to stand for Christ. Which is why at the Easter Vigil, the church starts in darkness. And the deacon sings light of Christ three times while the whole building lights up [music] candle by candle from a single flame.

And that's what your candle is. You pray for a few minutes and leave, but the flame keeps burning. It's your prayer staying there when you no longer can. A sign that what you asked for didn't go out when you walked through the door. Who did you light your last candle for?
