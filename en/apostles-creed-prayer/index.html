The Apostles' Creed: Pray It With Me

The apostles' creed: pray it with me now, everything Catholics believe in one short prayer, prayed since the first centuries of the Church.

Pray along with the video

Text of the prayer

The Church has prayed it since the first centuries.

I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth, and in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord, who was conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the Virgin Mary, suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died and was buried; he descended into hell; on the third day he rose again from the dead; he ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty; from there he will come to judge the living and the dead. I believe in the Holy Spirit, the holy catholic Church, the communion of saints, the forgiveness of sins, the resurrection of the body, and life everlasting. Amen.

Lord, increase my faith.

What the Bible says

The Apostles' Creed is the ancient baptismal creed of the Church of Rome, and tradition says it sums up the faith of the twelve apostles. It professes God the Father Creator, Jesus Christ born of the Virgin Mary, crucified, risen and coming again, the Holy Spirit, the Church, the forgiveness of sins and life everlasting.

What the Church teaches

The Catechism is built around the Creed: it calls it "the faithful witness of the faith of the Church of Rome" and explains its twelve articles one by one (Catechism 194-196). The Apostles' Creed is also the first prayer of the Rosary, and the Nicene Creed, a longer version, is the one we usually profess at Sunday Mass.
