Saint Maximilian Kolbe: The Priest Who Volunteered to Die in Another Man's Place

A prisoner was selected for death at Auschwitz. A priest stepped forward and said: take me instead. That man lived. The priest became a saint.

In July 1941, a prisoner escaped from Auschwitz. The Nazis selected 10 men to die of starvation as punishment. One of them, Franciszek Gajowniczek, cried out for his wife and children. A Franciscan priest named Maximilian Kolbe stepped forward: "I am a Catholic priest. I want to take his place." The commander agreed. Kolbe spent two weeks in the starvation bunker — praying, singing hymns, comforting the dying. When the guards returned, the other nine were dead. Kolbe was still alive. They killed him with a lethal injection. He was canonized by Pope John Paul II in 1982. The man he saved lived to see it.

What the Church teaches

🙏 Saint Maximilian Kolbe is one of the most powerful examples of Christian love in the 20th century. In July 1941, he stepped forward in Auschwitz and offered his life in place of a stranger — a man he had never met — simply because that man had a wife and children waiting for him. Kolbe spent two weeks in the starvation bunker, praying, singing hymns and comforting the other condemned men. When the guards returned after two weeks, nine men had died. Kolbe was still alive, kneeling in prayer. They killed him with a lethal injection on August 14, 1941 — the eve of the Feast of the Assumption. The man he saved, Franciszek Gajowniczek, lived to attend Kolbe's canonization in 1982. He outlived the Nazi regime. He outlived the Soviet era. He died in 1995 at the age of 93. Maximilian Kolbe's love was not an emotion. It was a decision.
