She Was a Foreign Widow — and Ended Up in the Family Line of Jesus | Ruth

Ruth was a foreigner, a widow, with no visible future. She became the great-grandmother of King David — in the direct line of Jesus Christ. Discover the story of Ruth and Naomi, the Bible's most beautiful lesson on loyalty.

When Naomi lost her husband and sons and decided to return to Israel, she told Ruth: 'Go back to your people.' Ruth's answer became one of the most beautiful vows in the Old Testament: 'Where you go, I will go; your people shall be my people, and your God my God.'

What the video says

Ruth was a foreigner, but widow, with no visible future. She became the great grandmother of King David. This is a story about loyalty, faith, and redemption. When Naomi lost her husband and sons and decided to return to Israel, she told Ruth, go back to your people.

Ruth's response became the most beautiful words of loyalty in all of Scripture, where you go, I will go. Your people will be my people, and your God, my God. That radical loyalty was rewarded. Ruth met Boaz, married, had a son named Obed, father of Jesse, father of King David.

Loyalty to God, even when you don't see the plan, always leads to blessing. Who are you called to be faithful to today?

What the Church teaches

📖 The story of Ruth and Naomi is one of the most beautiful in the Bible, showing loyalty, faith, and redemption. Ruth, a foreign widow with no visible future, chose to stay with her mother-in-law Naomi instead of returning to her own people, saying the unforgettable words: "Where you go, I will go." That faithfulness led her to Bethlehem, where God rewarded her humility: she married Boaz and became the great-grandmother of King David, placing her directly in the family line of Jesus Christ. This story teaches us that God does not look at our background or circumstances, but at the faithfulness of our heart. Ruth had nothing material to offer, but her loyalty and trust in God made her a key piece in the story of salvation.
