The St. Benedict Medal The Hidden Exorcism Prayer Most People Wear Unknowingly

It is one of the most powerful medals in the Church — and most people who wear it have no idea what its letters mean. Discover the hidden meaning of the Saint Benedict Medal.

Its initials spell out an entire exorcism prayer in Latin: "Step back, Satan! Never tempt me with your vanities. What you offer me is evil — drink the poison yourself!" It recalls the day enemies tried to poison Saint Benedict and the cup shattered at his blessing.

Text of the prayer

It's one of the most powerful medals in the church, and most people who wear it have no idea what those letters actually mean. Hidden in tiny initials around the edge is an entire exorcism prayer, a command against evil that Catholics have carried for centuries.

Once you know what it says, you'll never look at this medal the same way. On the back, the letters V R S NS MM V stand for a Latin command. Step back, Satan. Never tempt me with your vanities. What you offer me is evil.

Drink the poison yourself. It recalls the day jealous monks tried to poison St. Benedict. When he made the sign of the cross [music] over the cup, it shattered in his hands. Around the edge, more letters repeat. May the holy cross be my light. Let not [music] the dragon be my guide.

But hear this clearly. The medal is not a lucky charm. Its power comes from the blessing of the church and your faith in Christ's victory over evil. It's a prayer you wear, a daily reminder to reject darkness and choose Christ. That's why the faithful place it in their homes, their cars, even the foundations of their houses, not out of fear, but as a constant prayer that wherever they go, the cross of Christ goes before them.

So wear it with faith, not as a charm, but as a prayer. Did you know what those letters meant?

What the Church teaches

The Saint Benedict Medal carries one of the most powerful prayers of protection in the Catholic Church. The letters V-R-S-N-S-M-V around the cross stand for 'Vade retro Satana' — 'Step back, Satan! Never tempt me with your vanities. What you offer me is evil — drink the poison yourself!' It recalls the day jealous monks tried to poison Saint Benedict, and the cup shattered when he blessed it. But remember what the Church teaches: the medal is not a lucky charm. Its power comes from the blessing of the Church and your faith in Christ's victory over evil.
