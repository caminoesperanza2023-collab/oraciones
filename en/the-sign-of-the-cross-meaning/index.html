The Sign of the Cross: What Catholics Are Really Declaring

Catholics make the Sign of the Cross dozens of times every week. But do we know what we're actually saying?

In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit — in one gesture, the entire Christian faith. The early Christians used it as their mark of identity even under persecution.

Watch the video

What the video says

Catholics make the sign of the cross dozens of times every week. But how often do we stop to think about what we are actually doing? The gesture traces the shape of the cross on our body from forehead to chest, from shoulder to shoulder. With those movements, we are declaring the entire Christian faith in a single gesture.

In the name of the Father, we acknowledge that God is our creator and our origin. And of the Son, we profess that Jesus Christ, the second person of the Trinity, became man and died for us. And of the Holy Spirit, we recognize the divine presence that lives within us.

The early Christians were identified by this gesture in times of persecution. It was a mark of belonging, a silent declaration. I belong to the crucified one. Next time you make the sign of the cross, slow down. Do it deliberately. Say each word as if it were the first time because every time is a renewal of your baptismal commitment.
