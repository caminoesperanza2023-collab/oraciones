Who Were the 12 apostles of Jesus? Their Lives, Mission and Martyrdom

Jesus chose twelve ordinary men to change the world: fishermen, a tax collector, a zealot. Peter, Andrew, James, John, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James, Thaddaeus, Simon and Judas. Discover who the twelve Apostles were, how they received the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, and how most of them died as martyrs for the truth they witnessed.

They had everything to lose and nothing to gain—except the truth: Jesus rose from the dead.

Watch the video

What the video says

Jesus chose twelve ordinary men to change the world. Fishermen, a tax collector, a zealot — no famous rabbis, no politicians. Peter, the rock on whom He built His Church. Andrew, the first called. James and John, the sons of thunder. Philip and Bartholomew. Matthew the tax collector.

Thomas, who doubted and then declared You are my Lord and my God. James son of Alphaeus. Thaddaeus. Simon the Zealot. And Judas Iscariot, who betrayed Him. After Judas, Matthias was chosen to complete the twelve. These were the men who received the Holy Spirit at Pentecost.

They went to the ends of the earth. Most died as martyrs. Peter was crucified upside down. Andrew on an X-shaped cross. Bartholomew was skinned alive. They had everything to lose and nothing to gain — except the truth they had witnessed. Jesus rose from the dead.

And they gave their lives to proclaim it.

What the Church teaches

🙏 Jesus chose twelve ordinary men, fishermen, a tax collector, a zealot, to change the world forever. The Apostles were not famous or powerful, yet after receiving the Holy Spirit at Pentecost they carried the Gospel to the ends of the earth, and most died as martyrs rather than deny what they had seen: that Jesus rose from the dead. Their courage proves the Resurrection is real, because no one dies for what they know to be a lie. God still calls ordinary people today to do extraordinary things for His Kingdom.
