The Eucharist: Why Catholics Believe It Is Truly the Body of Christ

At the Last Supper, Jesus said: This IS my Body. Not 'this represents' — this IS. Catholics believe that at every Mass, that same miracle happens again. Really. Truly.

John chapter 6 is unmistakable. When disciples left Jesus over this teaching, He didn't run after them to say it was symbolic. He let them go.

What the video says

At the last supper, Jesus took bread, blessed it, and said, "This is my body." He didn't say the bread represented his body. He said, "This is my body." Catholics believe that at every mass, that same miracle happens again. The bread becomes the body of Christ.

The wine becomes his blood. really truly substantially. This is called transsubstantiation. The substance of the bread and wine changes completely even though the outward appearance remains the same. John 6 is unmistakable. Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life. When many disciples left Jesus over this teaching, he didn't run after them to say it was just a symbol.

He let them go. Next time you receive communion, pause for a moment. Before you do, remember who you are receiving. This is not a ritual. It is a personal encounter with the living

What the Church teaches

This video is for you if you needed a reminder today that you are not alone. Faith is not believing everything will be perfect. It's believing God is present in everything — in what goes well and in what hurts. That changes everything. Saint Paul said it better than anyone ever could: "I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me." Not all the things I want. All things — including forgiving, waiting, getting back up, and moving forward. If this video found you today, it wasn't by accident. God speaks through the ordinary.
