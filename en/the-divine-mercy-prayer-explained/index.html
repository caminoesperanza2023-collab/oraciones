The Divine Mercy Prayer: Why Jesus Gave This to a Polish Nun in 1931

In 1935, Jesus appeared to a young Polish nun named Faustina Kowalska and made a promise so extraordinary that priests who read her diary initially refused to believe it was real: whoever recites the Divine Mercy chaplet will receive great mercy at the hour of death.

The chaplet is prayed on ordinary rosary beads, repeating 'For the sake of His sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world' — a reminder that Christ's suffering isn't just history, it's active mercy poured out for you right now. Jesus also told Faustina: 'I desire that the whole world know My mercy' — not just the good souls, the whole world, including you on your worst day. To her He said the line that defines the entire devotion: 'The greater the sinner, the greater the right to My mercy.' You are never too far gone.

Today at 3pm, the Hour of Mercy, pray simply: 'Jesus, I trust in You.'

What the Church teaches

In 1935, Jesus gave Saint Faustina Kowalska a promise so extraordinary that even priests hesitated to believe it: 'Whoever recites this chaplet will receive great mercy at the hour of death.' The Divine Mercy Chaplet is prayed on ordinary rosary beads, repeating: 'For the sake of His sorrowful Passion, have mercy on us and on the whole world.' And Jesus asked for one hour especially — 3 PM, the hour He died — the Hour of Mercy. His words to Faustina were clear: 'I desire that the whole world know My mercy.' Not just the good ones. The whole world. Including you on your worst day. Pray it today at 3 PM and simply say: 'Jesus, I trust in You.' Will you join? 🙏
