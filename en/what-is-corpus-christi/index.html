What Is Corpus Christi? The Catholic Feast That Defies Science

On Corpus Christi, Catholics process through streets carrying something they believe is God himself. Here's the history and theology behind this astonishing feast.

Watch the video

What the video says

Once a year, millions of Catholics carry Jesus through the streets of their cities. Not a statue, not a symbol, the real presence. Corpus Christi, Latin for body of Christ, is one of the most ancient feasts of the church. And it began with a vision that changed the Catholic calendar [music] forever.

In the 13th century, a Belgian nun named Juliana of Leazge kept seeing a vision, a full moon with a dark spot. God revealed to her that the dark spot represented something missing from the church's calendar, a feast dedicated entirely to the Eucharist. She spent years pleading with church leaders.

Finally, Pope Urban IV established Corpus Christi in 1264 and asked Thomas Aquinus, the greatest theologian who ever lived, to write the hymns. Aquinus wrote the Pangi Lingua, considered the most beautiful eucharistic hymn in history. Today when you see that golden monstrance carried through the streets, you are seeing 800 years of Catholic faith declaring in public Jesus is truly here.

Not his memory as presence. When Jesus is carried through the streets, he passes in front of every house, every broken family, every lonely person. Corpus [music] Christi is God saying I am not only in the church I am in your neighborhood. This Corpus Christi when you see the procession kneel.

It is not a tradition. It is an encounter.
