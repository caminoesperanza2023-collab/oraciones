A 14-year-old peasant girl who could not read went to a cave in southern France in 1858. The woman she saw there told her something so theologically precise that Bernardet had to repeat it over and over on the walk home just so she wouldn't forget words she didn't even understand.

Her name was Bernardet [music] Subaru and she had 18 apparitions at the grotto of Masabiel. [music] The church investigated for 4 years before officially confirming them. The message was not what anyone [music] expected. No promises, no comfort. First, the woman asked for penance, repeating that single word three times.

Penance, conversion, prayer. Then she told Bernardet to dig in the dirt with her bare hands. Bernardet did, and water began to flow from ground that had been completely dry. That spring has [music] never stopped flowing since. Over 7,000 cures have been documented at [music] Lords and 69 declared miraculous by the church after rigorous independent medical investigation.

Doctors trying to disprove them and failing. But the moment that silenced every skeptic came when Bernardet [music] finally asked the woman her name. She answered in the local dialect, I am the immaculate conception. Four years earlier, Pope Pius I 9th had officially defined that exact dogma.

Bernardet, a girl who had never studied theology, had no way of knowing those words even existed. Mary's message at Lords isn't just history. [music] Pray, do penance, come back to God. That's still [music] her message. And the water still flows today. Have you ever prayed with Lord's water?

What the Church teaches

At Lourdes in 1858, Our Lady appeared eighteen times to Bernadette Soubirous, a poor, sick, unschooled girl of fourteen. Her message was simple and it still stands: pray, do penance, come back to God. She asked for a spring to be uncovered — and that water still flows today, with over 70 medically documented cures that science cannot explain. But the greatest miracle of Lourdes is not physical healing: it is the millions who arrive broken and leave with peace. Mary never points to herself; she always leads us to her Son.
