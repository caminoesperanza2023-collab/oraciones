Saint Joan of Arc: The Teenage Girl Who Led an Army and Changed History

She was 17. She heard voices. She led an army. She was burned at the stake. And 500 years later, the Church made her a saint.

Joan of Arc was a French peasant girl who claimed to hear the voices of Saint Michael, Saint Catherine, and Saint Margaret — telling her to drive the English out of France. In 1429, she convinced the French crown prince to let her lead the army. She rode into battle, was wounded multiple times, and helped turn the tide of the Hundred Years War. She was captured, tried for heresy, and burned alive at 19. Twenty-five years later, the Church reopened the trial and declared her innocent. In 1920, Pope Benedict XV canonized her. She remains one of the most remarkable lives in all of human history.

A teenager changed the world because she listened to God. What's your excuse?
