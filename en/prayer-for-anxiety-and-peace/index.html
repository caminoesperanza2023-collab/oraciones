Prayer for Anxiety and Peace When Your Mind Will Not Rest

Pray for anxiety and peace when your thoughts feel crowded and your mind will not rest. Pause, breathe, and place this present moment in God's care without pretending that fear is not real.

Pray along with the video

Full text

This prayer will not deny what you feel. It will help you place this moment in God's care.

Lord Jesus, my mind feels crowded. Hold what I cannot carry. Give me wisdom for the next decision, courage for the next step, and peace for this breath. Remind me that fear is present, but it is not my master.

Stay with me, Lord. Let your presence become steadier than the noise inside me. Amen.

What the Bible says

Lord Jesus, hold what I cannot carry. Give me wisdom for the next decision, courage for the next step, and peace for this breath. Remind me that fear may be present, but it is not my master. Stay with me and let your presence become steadier than the noise within me.

What the Church teaches

First Peter 5:7 invites Christians to cast their anxieties on God because He cares for them. That verse does not shame a person for feeling anxious, and it does not teach that sincere prayer instantly removes every symptom. Biblical prayer makes room for truth: we can name fear, ask for help, and take the next responsible step while trusting that we are not abandoned. Catholic faith sees care for the mind and body as compatible with prayer. Speaking with a trusted family member, priest, counsellor, doctor, or emergency service when needed can be an act of courage and stewardship, not a failure of faith. In a difficult moment, slow the prayer down. Notice your breathing, name one concern rather than every possible future, and ask God for the grace required for the next decision. Peace may arrive as a feeling, but it can also appear as clarity, support, patience, or strength to seek help. Christ's presence does not make pain imaginary. It gives us a relationship in which pain can be spoken honestly. If someone shares anxiety with you, listen without giving simplistic promises. Offer companionship, practical support, prayer, and encouragement toward qualified care when appropriate.
