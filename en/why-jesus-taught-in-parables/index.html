Jesus Did not Use Parables to Make Things Simpler

The disciples asked Jesus to his face: why do you speak to them in parables? His answer wasn't «so they'll understand me better». It was almost the opposite.

In Matthew 13 he answers by quoting Isaiah. And Mark adds the detail that clears it up: he spoke to them «as they were able to hear it». A parable does something a direct lecture cannot — it gets in before you raise your guard.

The most brutal example is in the Old Testament. The prophet Nathan had to confront King David over his worst sin. He didn't accuse him: he told him a story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb. David erupted and passed a death sentence. Then Nathan said four words: «You are that man» (2 Samuel 12).
