Saint Padre Pio: The Priest Who Bore the Wounds of Christ for 50 Years

He read souls. He bilocated. He bore the wounds of Jesus Christ — the stigmata — for 50 years. And the Catholic Church declared him a saint.

Padre Pio received the stigmata in 1918 and carried them until his death in 1968. His confessional line stretched for weeks. He told penitents their sins before they spoke. Thousands of miracles were attributed to him.

On the night of his death, his wounds completely disappeared.

What the video says

He read souls. He bilocated. He bore the wounds of Jesus for 50 years. And the Catholic Church declared him a saint. Padre Pio of Pietrelcina received the stigmata in 1918 — the five wounds of Christ — and carried them until his death. His confessional line stretched for weeks.

People came from across the world to confess to him. He would tell penitents their sins before they said a word. Many wept and never forgot it. Thousands of miracles were attributed to him during his lifetime and after. He founded a hospital called the House for the Relief of Suffering.

He prayed for the sick day and night. On September 23, 1968, he died with the words: Jesus, Mary. His wounds had completely disappeared. Pray this now: Padre Pio, pray for us.

What the Church teaches

Saint Padre Pio bore the stigmata — the visible wounds of Christ — for 50 years, medically examined and never explained. But his greatest miracles happened in the confessional, where he spent up to 16 hours a day and could read souls, reminding penitents of sins they had forgotten. He founded the Home for the Relief of Suffering, one of Italy's great hospitals, saying suffering deserves both prayer AND medicine. His most famous words: 'Pray, hope, and don't worry.'
